How do you simplify a B2B journey inside a highly complex legacy system?

I redesigned the certification flows, kickstarted the company's foundational design system, and structured the hand-off process with the development team.

Context

Peterson Solutions certifies processes in agribusiness. The system was used daily by auditors, technicians, and managers with very different routines and experience levels.

The challenge

The project came about because the operational cost had become visible: an overloaded support team, difficulty retaining auditors, and a slow development cycle due to the lack of reusable components.

The main certification process required 47 clicks and 23 minutes. Not because the process itself was complex, but because the interface didn't follow the logic of the person performing the task.

What I did

I redesigned the flow in the order the user thinks, not the order the system was built. The design system was defined before any high-fidelity screen. There was pressure to show visual progress quickly, but without established tokens and components, any interface produced would be inconsistent with what engineering was already building in parallel.

The result

Satisfaction from 2.1 to 4.2. Support tickets from 87 to 23 per month. Main process completion time with 62% fewer clicks. New feature implementation time from 3 weeks to 2 weeks.

Intro

Data

The research was done together with the requirements team, which conducted user interviews, and through collaborative sessions with stakeholders where this material was analyzed.

Two points shaped the project from there.

The problem wasn't the number of clicks. It was the lack of feedback during the process. Users didn't know if they'd done something right until they hit an error a few steps later.

The second point was about profiles. Experienced auditors and technicians in training performed the same task with completely different mental models. The interface ignored this and treated both the same way.

Problem

  1. 01

    Complicated, Lengthy Flows

    The certification process required an average of 47 clicks and 23 minutes to complete, with a high rate of errors and rework due to a lack of contextual validation and clear feedback.

  2. 02

    Legacy, Confusing Interface

    Users reported only 2.1/5 satisfaction. Main complaints: difficulty finding features and understanding process status.

  3. 03

    No Standardization

    Without a design system, development created components in isolation, generating visual inconsistencies that made the code harder to maintain.

Solution

Three fundamental pillars that transformed the product experience and the team's efficiency.

Mapping with the requirements team

There were several conversations, facilitated sessions, and meetings with the requirements team and stakeholders to align on business definitions.

Understanding the journey from the user's perspective became the deciding factor when defining the function/task each screen was meant to accomplish.

Flow Simplification

I completely restructured the certification flows, rebuilding the journey understanding at the wireframe stage, cutting unnecessary steps, and reorganizing information based on users' mental models.

The main process was reduced from 47 to 18 clicks, with clear visual hierarchy and contextual feedback at every step.

Structured Hand-off Process

I created a hand-off process that transformed collaboration between design and development, with detailed technical spec documentation and production-ready organized assets.

Weekly alignment meetings between design and engineering reduced rework by 71% and sped up the implementation of new screens.

The problematic experience resulted in falling user productivity, a significant increase in support tickets (averaging 87/month), difficulty hiring and retaining auditors, and a slower development cycle due to the lack of reusable components and clear guidelines.

These numbers made it clear the problem wasn't just usability — it was a direct risk to the business, which is what made the redesign a strategic priority for the company.
